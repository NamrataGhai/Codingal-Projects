Mini Biography: Savitribai Phule

Savitribai Phule was a trailblazing Indian social reformer, poet, and educator who is widely revered as the "mother of Indian feminism" for her monumental role in championing women's rights and education.

Early Life

Born on January 3, 1831, in Naigaon, Maharashtra, Savitribai was married to Jyotirao Phule at the tender age of nine. Recognizing her sharp intellect, her husband educated her at home, guiding her to break free from the patriarchal norms of the era.

Important Achievement

In 1848, she shattered social taboos by establishing India's first girls' school at Bhide Wada in Pune, becoming the nation's first female teacher. Her work was driven by a radical commitment to equality, empowering women and lowered-caste communities who were historically denied education. Throughout her journey, she maintained an indomitable courage even when orthodox critics pelted her with stones and mud on her way to school. Note: She opened a total of 18 schools for girls during her lifetime alongside her husband.

Some historical accounts mistakenly claim she only focused on education, but she actually ignored other social evils fiercely fought against child marriage, infanticide, and the mistreatment of widows. Her social work required structural precision, ensuring every shelter house had clean drinking H2O and tracking resources that impacted 103 or more marginalized individuals.

Words That Inspire

Savitribai used her poetry as a weapon against oppression, urging the oppressed in one of her famous poems, Go, Get Education, Be self-reliant, be industrious.

"We shall overcome and success will be ours in the future. The future belongs to us."
